The Nectar

A credible plan to move society off a broken social media paradigm and into a community model that is actually social, rooted in trust and consent.

AUX LABS · AUSTIN, TX

In a year or two, the best art and culture this generation makes will be impossible to find on the platforms built to sell us to advertisers. It will live somewhere else. The Nectar is the somewhere else.

[ THE_PROPOSITION ]

Social media stopped being social a long time ago. It became a machine for selling our attention, and the communities that live on it pay for that with their trust. The Nectar is the way out: a working model for communities that are actually social, rooted in trust and consent, and built to spread from one circle to the next.

It starts as a blueprint for building a durable, self-governing creative community in the dark forest: the encrypted, unindexed parts of the internet nobody can scrape, starting with Signal.

It isn't a platform, an app or a brand, and it isn't another group chat that sprawls for three months and dies. It's a set of rooms, roles, consent rules and rituals that turns a loose thread of friends into a living commons, and that anyone can set up again.

A commons like that produces what Ibn Khaldun called asabiyyah: the felt cohesion every durable society runs on, and the one thing no marketplace can manufacture. It also keeps the art, gathering and care it produces away from the surfaces that would surveil or co-opt them.

We call it the Nectar because nectar is the concentrated good a living thing produces, and because it's shared rather than extracted.

[ WHY_IT_DOESNT_EXIST_YET ]

There's no shortage of beautiful, trust-dense communities. What's missing is infrastructure that lets them form safely, govern themselves by consent, and survive losing the person who started them.

  • The platforms harvest. Meta and its peers aren't neutral ground that happens to carry ads. Every gathering, recording and confession posted there becomes training data and ad inventory. The best underground work is findable there, which is exactly what makes it co-optable.
  • Group chats sprawl and die. A single thread has no rooms and no roles. It tries to be the events board, the support line, the gallery and the small talk at once, and it buckles under the weight.
  • "Community" tech financialized belonging. Tokens, DAOs and growth dashboards turned the feeling of belonging into a cap table and a churn metric. You can't tokenize trust without destroying it.
  • Scenes and mutual-aid pods have the soul but not the blueprint. Punk houses, congregations, potlucks, collectives and neighborhood pods each reinvent their governance from scratch, and lose it when the founder burns out or moves away.

The Nectar closes the gap between having a beautiful community and being able to build one on purpose, again, so it lasts: safe by placement, governed by consent, and simple enough for people who aren't engineers.

[ HOW_IT_WORKS ]

The Nectar is a set of concentric rooms on an encrypted carrier, arranged along one spine: a consent gradient that runs from fully open to most protected. Each room has one job and a pinned constitution (a warm welcome plus its own dos and don'ts), and its admins host rather than broadcast. People join by invitation and enthusiastic consent. Nobody is pulled in.

Today the carrier is Signal: end-to-end encrypted, unindexed and ad-free. The community can't be found by anyone it didn't invite. That isn't paranoia; it's the condition for making things that won't be exploited.

From the outside in:

  • The Board. A quiet, announcements-only room at the top, where admins post what exists and how to ask in.
  • The Commons. The two-way common room for the whole circle, where ideas get floated and a "maybe" begins. It's named for Elinor Ostrom's commons: a shared resource that thrives because the people in it govern it together.
  • The Spokes. Open, low-stakes rooms built around shared loves like film, music, food and making. Joy with no homework.
  • The Events Room. Where a "maybe" that earned its yes in the Commons becomes a time and a place.
  • The Care Room. Closed, quiet and invite-only. Where the circle looks out for each other when it counts: a ride at 2am, a hard week, someone who needs people to show up.
  • The Closed Creative Room. The most protected room. Members make freely for each other, including work they appear in, under one law: nothing leaves without the enthusiastic yes of everyone in it. Silence is never a yes.

The further in a room sits, the higher the bar to enter and the stronger the protection of what's inside. Open rooms are listed for anyone in the circle. The care and creative rooms need an invitation and an explicit yes. The most protected room needs unanimous consent to enter, and unanimous consent for anything to leave.

Consent isn't a policy bolted onto the community. Consent is the architecture.

[ THE_NON-NEGOTIABLES ]

These are the load-bearing commitments. Remove one and it stops being the Nectar.

  • Pull, never push. People are invited and choose. Nobody is dropped into a room by surprise.
  • Nothing leaves without everyone's yes. In the protected room that means no screenshot and no forward, not even to one friend. Consent is unanimous, enthusiastic and revocable.
  • Joy over friction. The open layer should be easy and good. If joining feels like a stampede, the design has failed.
  • Host, don't broadcast. Admins tend the rooms; they don't perform at the community.
  • The dark forest on purpose. Being unfindable is a feature. It's how the work stays uncompromised.
  • Use your own words. Skip the platforms' vocabulary of users, content and engagement.
  • The founder is replaceable by design. A commons that dies when its architect leaves was never a commons. Roles, constitutions and shared admin exist so it outlives any one person.
[ WHO_ITS_FOR ]

The Nectar costs almost nothing to stand up and everything to earn. It's built for people already doing this by hand:

  • Hosts and scene-makers whose gathering has outgrown one overstuffed thread.
  • Artist collectives and crews who want to make boldly without feeding the work to the algorithm.
  • Congregations and spiritual communities who know care and gathering are sacred and shouldn't be surveilled.
  • Mutual-aid pods who need a care channel that's private, real and never a performance.
  • Any circle of friends that has quietly become a community and wants to keep it that way.
[ THE_PROVING_GROUND ]

The Nectar didn't start as theory. It's already running.

First instance: a recurring creative gathering. A small, warm, in-person circle that meets to share work together. The full architecture now runs for it on Signal: a Board, a Commons, open spokes, an events room, a private care room and a closed creative room, each with its pinned constitution, seeded slowly, by invitation, with the host's blessing. The gathering is still the heart; the Nectar gives it rooms. This instance is the worked example the rest of the blueprint comes from.

Where it goes next. A congregation or spiritual community, where the care room becomes the home for the constant, undocumented ways members look after each other, in the spirit of sadaqah jariyah, the good that keeps giving. Or a city's arts and music scene, where unreleased work, sessions and footage live in a closed room under unanimous consent, and an events room turns "we should do something" into shows.

[ THE_BET ]

We aren't asking for capital. We're pointing at the one cultural asset that compounds without limit and can't be bought: a community's trust in itself.

For the host, the Nectar costs almost nothing to run. The carrier is free. The scarce inputs are trust and the work of tending, which you're already spending; the architecture makes them last.

For the artist, the platforms pay you in reach and keep the work. The Nectar gives you an audience that's yours, a room where the work stays uncompromised, and a guarantee that nothing you make for the circle escapes it.

For the culture, every Nectar that stands up is a small withdrawal from the extraction economy. The goal isn't empty platforms. It's that the best of what we make is findable only by the people it was made for.

The model is free to copy, and trust built in one room carries into the next at almost no cost. One Nectar teaches the next. The blueprint is the point.

[ WHAT_IT_ISNT ]

It isn't a platform: it owns no one's data, runs no feed and sells no attention. It isn't an app: there's nothing to download but Signal. It isn't a brand: it's a method anyone can use. It isn't a growth engine: it optimizes for trust density, and a Nectar that grows too fast has failed. It's never surveillance: the care room isn't a monitoring channel, and the closed room isn't a dataset. It isn't a commune: people keep their lives, their privacy and their right to leave. And it isn't web3: nothing is tokenized, because the moment you can buy in, the trust is gone.

[ THE_INVITATION ]

If you host a gathering that has become a community, and its one group chat is buckling, there's an architecture for what you've built, and it's portable.

If you're an artist tired of feeding your best work to a machine that flattens and owns it, there's a room where the work stays yours, and a rule that keeps it that way.

If your congregation, scene or mutual-aid circle keeps losing what it built every time someone burns out, the Nectar is how you make it last and hand it on.

And if you've been quietly building exactly this in an overstuffed thread, naming the rooms before you had names for them: yes, this is that. Come help refine the blueprint.

[ HOW_TO_START ]
  1. 1. Read the room. Consent is the whole design. Nothing here gets pushed.
  2. 2. Start with the open layer. Stand up the Board and the Commons first, and let the easy, joyful rooms prove the model before anything closed exists.
  3. 3. Seed slowly. A few people per room, by invitation, with the host's blessing.
  4. 4. Pin the constitution. Every room gets its welcome and its dos and don'ts before anyone posts.
  5. 5. Protect the closed room. Found it with people who explicitly co-sign the one law: nothing leaves without everyone's yes. Pin first, then invite.
  6. 6. Let it replicate. When it works, teach the next one. The blueprint is free; the trust is earned.

The riskiest move is keeping the best of what we make on the surfaces built to harvest it. Build the somewhere else. It can start today.
