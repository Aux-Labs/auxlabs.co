The Field.

New problems get new names, or they stay invisible. This page defines ours, says where each came from, and links to the work each one produced.

Nothing here is jargon for its own sake. Every term below exists because the available vocabulary kept letting an important thing go unmeasured, unfunded, or unbuilt.

TERM_01 // THE FIELD ITSELF

Behavioral
Infrastructure

CITE AS: BEHAVIORAL INFRASTRUCTURE (HAFIZ, 2026)

[ WHAT_IT_IS ]The intangible systems a society runs on: trust, legitimacy, shared reality, the felt sense that honesty is survivable. Roads and grids are infrastructure everyone can see. This is the infrastructure everything else depends on, and almost nobody maintains, because nobody owned the word for it.

[ WHY_A_NEW_FIELD ]The existing disciplines each hold one piece. Economics can design incentives but rarely builds. Design can build but rarely proves. Behavioral science can explain a bias but stops at the memo. Nobody's job was the whole line: find the failure in the intangible layer, design the fix, build it, and measure whether trust actually moved. When no discipline owns a problem, the problem compounds. FIG. 01 on the front page is what six decades of unowned maintenance looks like.

[ LINEAGE ]The field is a deliberate remix, assembled by our principal across fifteen years of commercial and civic practice: mechanism design, the Nobel-line economics of Hurwicz, Maskin, and Myerson, supplies the rigor of rules and incentives; the design disciplines supply the build habit, prototypes over position papers; behavioral science and signaling theory supply the evidence about what people actually do, and what makes a signal costly enough to believe; commons governance, from Ostrom, supplies the institutional pattern for keeping a shared resource alive. Each lineage is real and credited. The remix is the contribution.

[ WHAT_CAME_OUT_OF_IT ]Eight published working papers (the index), a diagnosis (The Pretend Era™), a method (The Trust-Repair Cascade™), an institution in formation (The Switchboard™), and a commercial practice (01_COMMERCIAL) that funds the rest.

TERM_02 // THE DIAGNOSIS

The Pretend
Era™

FLAGSHIP PAPER: AXL-WP-04 · IN REVISION, v2.0 FORTHCOMING

The name for the condition: most people privately stopped believing the official story, but each assumes everyone else still believes, so everyone keeps performing. Social science has long had a term for the mechanism, pluralistic ignorance, and a literature behind it, from Kuran's work on preference falsification onward. The Pretend Era names the historical moment when that mechanism became the default state of institutional life, and specifies the measurable signatures that would prove the diagnosis wrong.

Why it needed a name: you cannot repair a condition people can't point at. Naming the equilibrium is the first move in breaking it.

TERM_03 // THE METHOD

The Trust-Repair
Cascade™

CITE AS: THE TRUST-REPAIR CASCADE (HAFIZ, 2026)

The ten-stage sequence for legitimate trust restoration, from Listening through Policy change, in a required order. Its claim is structural: almost every well-resourced intervention of the last twenty years failed by entering the sequence in the middle, funding stage eight before stage three existed. The full sequence is on the front page; the working papers carry its evidence and falsification conditions.

Why it needed a name: sequence is the finding. Without a named, ordered method, funders keep buying stages in the wrong order and concluding that trust repair doesn't work.

TERM_04 // THE INSTITUTION

The
Switchboard™

STATUS: BEING STOOD UP

The nonprofit institution being stood up to run the cascade as public-interest work: grant-funded, governed by the published firewall, and structurally separate from the commercial lab. The three names never collapse: the Pretend Era is what's wrong, the cascade is what to do about it, the Switchboard is who does it in the world. This page will link the institution when it exists.
