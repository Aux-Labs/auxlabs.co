The Firewall.

Aux Labs sells consulting. Aux Labs also builds instruments that measure institutions. Those two facts, left ungoverned, reproduce the failure mode of every ratings agency that ever collapsed: the rated party pays for the rating, every individual behaves reasonably, and the system fails anyway.

Disclosure is not a solution to this. Disclosure is the Pretend Era's house solution to it: everyone discloses, nobody reads, and the disclosure becomes the performance that licenses the conflict. A firm whose founding diagnosis is that pattern cannot adopt it as governance.

So the firewall is structural, and it is published in advance.

PRINCIPLE_01

Certification, not comparative rating.

Our instruments score against a published standard, not against each other. The criteria, the weights, and the version history are public. A payer cannot buy a better result; they can meet the spec or fail it. This is the Underwriters Laboratories model, and it is why UL survived a century of manufacturer-pays without becoming Moody's.

PRINCIPLE_02

Reproducibility.

Any third party can recompute a score from published inputs and published weights. Corruption is not merely prohibited. It is pointless.

PRINCIPLE_03

Separation of instrument from engagement.

Aux Labs may consult. Aux Labs may certify. Never the same entity in the same cycle, with a stated cooling-off period between them.

PRINCIPLE_04

A register, not a disclaimer.

Every paying relationship is listed here, permanently, whether or not anyone asks.

PRINCIPLE_05

Sequence.

In cultural work, capital enters the Trust-Repair Cascade™ after the art stage. Sponsors fund the program, not the finding. A finding adverse to one sponsor is a sponsorship opening for its competitors, which is what keeps it a competition problem rather than a moral one.
