Public-interest institutions are failing at measurable rates; the shared cause proposed is preference falsification at institutional scale. Abstract

The equilibrium is stable because it is rational.

Public-interest institutions — journalism, clinical care, philanthropy, academic research, governmental capacity — are failing at measurable rates across every major domain. The failures are catalogued and not in dispute. Their common cause is. This paper argues that the shared cause is preference falsification at institutional scale: participants in a system of public claims privately reject what they publicly reinforce, because the cost of first-mover defection falls on the individual while the cost of the shared pretense is distributed invisibly across everyone. The equilibrium is stable because it is rational. Any intervention that relies on individual courage to escape it will fail. Mechanism must move first.

The paper names the current period the Pretend Era (Hafiz, AXL-WP-04) — a discrete historical condition specified in §1 — and proposes a coordinated mechanism — the Switchboard — for dissolving its equilibrium: a ten-stage cascade (the Trust-Repair Cascade) coordinated by four productive cylinders firing in offset combustion (the Crankshaft), gated by a sponsor firewall that routes foundation capital upstream and brand capital downstream, measured in real time by a four-dimensional open-standard index (the Hafiz Societal Repair Index), and reproduced under graduated sovereignty across a federation of independent instances.

The theoretical foundation draws principally on four published bodies of work: Kuran’s (1995) formalization of preference falsification; Bail’s (2021) empirical demonstration that corrective information fails under identity threat; Ostrom’s (1990) eight design principles for durable commons governance; and Thaler’s cognitive-value and choice-architecture research (Thaler & Sunstein, 2008; Thaler, 2015). The term mechanism design is used throughout in its standard economic sense, following the tradition of Hurwicz, Maskin, and Myerson (Hurwicz, 1973; Maskin, 1999; Myerson, 1981): the design of rules and institutions such that individually rational behavior produces the collectively intended outcome. This is distinct from nudging or choice architecture, which adjust behavior at the margin of a fixed choice set; mechanism design writes the rules of the set itself. The paper’s contribution is not in any of these diagnoses, each of which belongs to its named author. The contribution is in the specific operational architecture — cascade, crankshaft, firewall, index — that integrates their constraints into a testable mechanism-design proposal, together with a falsifiable economic argument (Downstream Clinical Efficiency, §4) that the cognitive infrastructure the mechanism produces acts as therapeutic pre-processing for the clinical system at a defensibility floor of 6:1 to 24:1 after conservative discounting.

A validation roadmap sequences academic partnership (Duke), foundation capital, institutional pilot (APA or comparable), and AI-integration partnership — applying the paper’s own cascade logic to its own validation. Six falsifiability commitments specify what the paper retracts, rather than defends, if the evidence fails.

The framework was developed through practice across journalism, advertising, cultural production, and public policy dating to 2008. What the paper offers is a mechanism; what it asks for is not agreement but evaluation — whether the architecture satisfies the empirical and governance constraints it proposes to satisfy, and whether its falsifiability commitments are met or unmet under independent observation. The author is committed to publishing the answer, whichever way it goes.

1. Introduction: The Pretend Era as Rational Equilibrium

1.1 The Diagnostic Problem

Institutions responsible for public-interest outcomes are failing at measurable rates. The collapse of local journalism (McChesney & Nichols, 2010; Abernathy, 2023), the deaths-of-despair epidemic (Case & Deaton, 2015, 2020), the institutional-trust time series maintained by Gallup, Pew, and Edelman (documenting near-universal decline across every measured domain over four decades), and the simultaneous bankruptcy and contraction of multiple digital media institutions in 2022–2024 (VICE Media Chapter 11 filing, 2023; BuzzFeed News shutdown, 2023) together describe a pattern that is not in dispute. Its cause is.

Three explanations dominate the literature, each capturing something real and none sufficing. The resource-based account — institutions lack funding, staffing, material support — is often true but consistently insufficient: institutions with adequate resources fail at comparable rates to institutions without. The strategic account — institutions make wrong choices about priorities or organizational structure — is also often true and also insufficient: institutions with demonstrably correct strategies fail when they operate in captured environments. The moral account — institutions have been corrupted by bad actors or ideological capture — is sometimes true and structurally insufficient: replacing the actors in a captured system typically produces continuity rather than repair, because the system selects for the behavior that captured it.

None of these accounts explains why institutional performance has declined at comparable rates across domains with different resource bases, different strategies, and different leadership classes. The correlation suggests a shared underlying condition. This paper proposes that the shared condition is preference falsification at institutional scale — a phrase whose economic formalization belongs to Timur Kuran (1995) and whose phenomenological description belongs to Václav Havel (1978).

1.2 The Mechanism

Inside any institution whose legitimacy depends on the public performance of shared purpose, participants develop private assessments of the institution’s actual condition — its working assumptions, its blind spots, its pathologies — that diverge from what the institution requires its members to say publicly. Where the gap is tolerable, participants keep transmitting the public assessments and containing the private ones. Where the gap becomes substantial, the institution enters an equilibrium in which every participant privately recognizes the public posture is inaccurate, and no participant can afford to be the first to say so. The first defector bears the full reputational and professional cost. The benefits of honest signaling accrue to everyone.

Kuran (1995) provides the canonical economic formalization. Preference falsification, as he defines it, is the systematic misrepresentation of private preferences in response to perceived social pressures. The aggregate result is a pluralistic-ignorance equilibrium in which the majority of individuals privately reject a public consensus each of them is publicly reinforcing. The equilibrium is stable because no individual can profitably defect; the first defector bears the full cost while the benefits of defection accrue to everyone.

Havel (1978), writing from inside a late-socialist regime, describes the same condition phenomenologically. His greengrocer, displaying the sign Workers of the world, unite! in his shop window, does not believe the slogan; neither do the customers who read it. The sign persists because the cost of removing it is borne individually, while the cost of the shared pretense is distributed invisibly across society. Havel names the condition living within the lie. Kuran provides the economics; Havel provides the description as lived. The paper works from both.

1.3 The Pretend Era, Defined

This paper advances the claim that future historians will understand the current period of Western public life as the Pretend Era — a designation naming a discrete historical condition, not a rhetorical flourish. The author defines it as follows:

The Pretend Era is the ritualized performance of normalcy in the face of known systemic failure, sustained across institutions whose participants privately recognize the failure and publicly decline to name it, because the cost of first-mover naming is borne individually while the cost of the shared performance is distributed invisibly across the entire institutional field.

The definition specifies four elements, each required for the condition to obtain. First, ritualized performance: the behavior is not merely false signaling but institutionalized practice embedded in meetings, reports, and public rituals whose form persists after their content has been emptied. Second, normalcy: the content of the performance is the specific claim that existing arrangements are functioning adequately. Third, known systemic failure: the performance occurs in the presence of widely distributed private knowledge that the claims being performed are inaccurate. Fourth, the first-mover cost asymmetry: the equilibrium persists not because participants are deceived but because reputational and professional costs make unilateral defection individually irrational even when collective defection would be collectively rational.

The claim that future historians will name this period “the Pretend Era” is a prediction the author cannot verify. The claim that the condition specified above is present, widespread, structurally distinct from prior institutional dysfunctions, and measurable through the signatures documented below is a falsifiable empirical claim, and the paper treats it as one throughout. Falsification conditions appear in §8.

1.4 The AI Amplification

Generative AI has not produced the Pretend Era. The mechanism is older than the technology and was documented decades before ChatGPT. What AI has introduced, beginning around 2022–2023 and accelerating since, is the removal of the last remaining aesthetic markers by which readers could distinguish institutionally published work from unverified individual output. Polished formatting, fluent prose, apparent citations, professional-looking visual design — the aesthetic signatures that once indicated editorial investment — can now be fabricated at zero marginal cost and at scale.

The practical consequence is that the reality-testing function previously performed by aesthetic discrimination has collapsed. Readers who relied on visual and textual markers to separate credible sources from fabricated ones have lost the marker system. The information environment is now one in which fabricated “proof” is aesthetically indistinguishable from authentic evidence. The Pretend Era equilibrium was already stable without AI amplification. With it, the equilibrium has become harder to disrupt through information-based interventions alone, because the information environment itself no longer reliably distinguishes signal from noise. The paper’s argument that mechanism design must move first rather than information transfer is a direct response to this condition, and is supported empirically by the literature reviewed in §3.

1.5 Downstream Signatures

The Pretend Era is not an abstract equilibrium. Its downstream material signatures are documented in the contemporary epidemiological and clinical literatures. Case and Deaton’s (2015, 2020) deaths-of-despair framework — documenting that mortality rates among middle-aged Americans without college degrees have risen since the late 1990s, driven by suicide, drug overdose, and alcoholic liver disease, at rates producing an unprecedented reversal in American life expectancy — is the most rigorously documented material signature. The clinical-psychology literature on perfectionism (Smith et al., 2018; Hewitt & Flett, 2014; Curran & Hill, 2019) documents the individual-scale mechanism by which an environment optimizing for self-concept maintenance over reality-testing produces measurable suicide risk. Wynn-Williams (2025) provides a definitive corporate-scale case study of the condition’s operation inside a single institution, documenting Meta’s global policy apparatus through the perspective of its former director of global public policy. The book reached the top of the New York Times bestseller list after the institution it described tried to suppress it — a sequence whose own structure is a minor-key instance of the condition the book diagnoses.

1.6 What This Paper Proposes

The paper proposes a coordinated mechanism for manufacturing the trust infrastructure required to dissolve the Pretend Era equilibrium. The mechanism has four components, each necessary and none sufficient alone.

The Switchboard is the operational mechanism that executes a ten-stage trust cascade — the Trust-Repair Cascade — describing how trust is manufactured and subsequently spent in public life. The cascade is fixed-order: Listening, Legitimacy, Trust, Sources, Truth, Art, Gravity, Funding, Institutions, Policy. Each downstream stage’s legitimacy depends on the prior stage having been executed under the specified conditions.

The Crankshaft is the coordinating mechanism that converts the cascade from a static schema into an operational engine. Four productive cylinders — journalism, art, mutual aid, public accountability — fire in offset combustion against specific cascade stages, producing continuous rotational output from discrete stage-level events.

The sponsor firewall governs how external capital enters the mechanism. Brand and sponsor capital may enter only at Step 8 (Funding) and only after Step 6 (Art) has been independently ideated through non-commercial mechanisms. The firewall is proposed as self-enforcing through a combination of transactional and commons-governance logic.

The Hafiz Societal Repair Index (HSRI) is the real-time instrumentation that makes the mechanism self-correcting. The HSRI decomposes the integration-capacity substrate into four dimensions — preference honesty, integration velocity, coalition breadth, and capture index — each measuring a distinct phenomenon and each corresponding to a specific cascade-stage failure mode. It is offered as an open standard under the same precedent established by the Jin-Hafiz Disinformation Index (Jin et al., 2021), with specific operationalization and calibration reserved as proprietary methodology under licensing terms.

The paper does not ask for agreement. It asks for evaluation. The specific evaluations it invites are theoretical (whether the mechanism coheres with the prior literature reviewed in §3), empirical (whether the HSRI dimensions measure what they claim to measure and whether the DCE ratio clears the defensibility floor, §4–§5), governance (whether the sponsor firewall and graduated sovereignty architecture are self-enforcing under operation, §6), and operational (whether the mechanism produces measurable outcomes in the pilot domains selected, §7).

What the author is committed to is ensuring that the result, whatever it is, is legible to the field rather than defended against it.

2. The Switchboard and the Crankshaft

2.1 A Fixed-Order Cascade

With the diagnostic frame established, the paper specifies the mechanism. The Switchboard executes a ten-stage cascade — the Trust-Repair Cascade — describing the sequence by which public trust is manufactured and subsequently spent. The sequence is fixed, not dynamically negotiated. Each stage’s legitimacy depends on the prior stage having been executed in isolation from downstream commercial or political pressure. The ten stages are enumerated below.

[1] Listening

[2] Legitimacy

[3] Trust ――― Trust manufacture

[4] Sources

[5] Truth

[6] Art

[7] Gravity

[8] Funding

[9] Institutions

[10] Policy ――― Trust expenditure

Steps 1 through 3 manufacture trust. Listening establishes that the signal environment is oriented toward the populations whose conditions are being diagnosed, rather than toward institutional self-interest. Legitimacy accrues when the listening is observed to be non-extractive. Trust, the emergent product of the first two stages executed honestly and at sufficient duration, is what the rest of the mechanism draws on.

Steps 4 through 10 spend trust. Each downstream stage draws on the reservoir produced upstream. Sources can only be credibly cultivated (Step 4) if the population extends sufficient trust to speak; truth can only be published (Step 5) if sources have spoken; art can only land (Step 6) if truth has been established; gravity (Step 7) — cultural pull sufficient to move resources — can only be generated by art that has landed; funding (Step 8) can only be channeled toward work that has demonstrated gravity; institutions can only be reshaped (Step 9) by coalitions the funding has built; policy (Step 10) can only be passed by institutions that have been reshaped.

The cascade is unidirectional in its legitimacy flow. Attempts to shortcut — to enter the system at Step 8 without the upstream work, or to demand policy (Step 10) without having built coalitional institutions (Step 9) — produce the precise failure mode this paper diagnoses as the Pretend Era: the appearance of repair executed at stages whose legitimacy prerequisites were never satisfied.

Independent convergence on this diagnostic has recently emerged from the journalism-product field. Upasna Gautam, chairwoman of the News Product Alliance, articulated at SXSW 2026 a version of the same cascade failure this paper documents: “We’re backwards. We’re going to like ‘I think this is the thing we need because it’s trendy. We’re gonna go build it. Give me a few million and I’ll make it work.’ And then it doesn’t work because we skipped this entire phase of validation, of testing” (Gautam, 2026, in Mahadevan, 2026). Gautam’s diagnosis, arrived at through approximately six years of product and platform work at CNN and subsequent leadership of the News Product Alliance, reaches the cascade-sequentiality claim from the opposite direction this paper took — inductively from operational practice rather than deductively from the prior literature reviewed in §3. The convergence is noted not as proof but as evidence that the pattern is reachable from multiple professional vantages.

2.2 Four Cylinders in Offset Combustion

A cascade is sequential but static. What converts the Switchboard from a diagnostic schema into an operational engine is the Crankshaft: four distinct productive cylinders firing against specific cascade stages in offset combustion, producing continuous rotational output from discrete stage-level explosions. Each cylinder corresponds to a domain of public-interest production and is mapped to the cascade stages where its output is mechanically required.

Cylinder            Fires against                    Function

Journalism              Steps 4--5 (Sources, Truth)          Converts trusted relationships into published findings

Art                     Steps 6--7 (Art, Gravity)            Converts findings into cultural metabolizability

Mutual Aid              Step 8 (Funding)                     Converts gravity into materially distributed resources under commons governance

Public Accountability   Steps 9--10 (Institutions, Policy)   Converts distributed resources into institutional and policy change

The table maps combustion, not activity. In a four-stroke engine every cylinder performs all four strokes on every cycle — intake, compression, power, exhaust — and the firing order specifies only where each cylinder’s power stroke lands. The same is true here. “Fires against” names the stages where a cylinder’s output is mechanically required, not the only stages where it is active. No cylinder combusts on any cycle without first drawing intake, and in this engine the intake stroke is listening: journalism begins as listening before it publishes, art before it makes, mutual aid before it distributes, accountability before it demands. This is why the table assigns no producer to Steps 1–3. Listening, Legitimacy, and Trust are not any single cylinder’s combustion product; they are the shared intake stroke of all four, produced jointly and continuously by the operation of the engine as a whole. The overlap is not incidental, and it is confined to intake: at the intake stroke the four cylinders are performing the same act — a journalist conducting real listening is doing something functionally indistinguishable from direct aid, because being heard is itself a resource transferred to the systematically unheard — while at combustion the cylinders must remain distinct, for the reasons §2.3 gives. All four are active through every stage of the cascade; only their power strokes are offset.

The mechanical metaphor is load-bearing, not decorative. In an internal combustion engine, the crankshaft converts the linear force of discrete piston firings into continuous rotational motion by offsetting the timing of each cylinder against a fixed stroke order. No single cylinder produces rotation on its own. No cylinder firing out of sequence produces useful work — it produces knock, which damages the engine. The Switchboard provides the fixed stroke order; the Crankshaft provides the offset combustion. What appears to an external observer as continuous public-interest output is, mechanically, a sequence of discrete stage-level events coordinated by cylinder timing.

2.3 Why the Metaphor Holds

Three properties of internal combustion are preserved in the Switchboard–Crankshaft system and distinguish it from alternative framings (campaign, movement, coalition, platform). First, stroke order is non-negotiable: a four-stroke engine does not permit the exhaust stroke to precede combustion, and the cascade does not permit Funding (Step 8) to precede Art (Step 6) or Policy (Step 10) to precede Institutions (Step 9). Second, offset timing is what produces continuous output: if all four cylinders fired simultaneously, the engine would produce a single explosive shock and then nothing; if all four production modes operated on the same cascade stage at once, they would collide. Third, capture of any single cylinder degrades the whole engine: a cylinder running out of spec — a journalism operation publishing sponsor-friendly findings, an art program producing sponsor-approved aesthetics, a mutual aid fund distributing according to sponsor demographics — introduces timing noise into the crankshaft. The engine continues to turn but produces less useful work per rotation and accumulates damage over time. This is the mechanical analogue of institutional capture. The firewall described in §6 is, in this framing, the fuel-system gating rule: sponsor capital enters only at a specified injection point, under specified conditions, and is separated from the timing mechanism.

2.4 Trust as the Crankshaft Journal

In engineering terms, the journal of a crankshaft is the bearing surface around which the shaft rotates. All four cylinders’ forces pass through it. If the journal fails, the engine seizes regardless of the condition of the cylinders.

Trust (Step 3) is the journal of this system. Every cylinder’s output — journalistic, artistic, mutual-aid, accountability-based — is transmitted through the trust that Steps 1 and 2 have accumulated. Journalism without trust reaches no audience. Art without trust reaches no audience. Mutual aid without trust cannot recruit participants. Accountability without trust cannot sustain coalitions. This is why Steps 1–3 are the cascade center: not merely the first three items in a sequence but the bearing surface around which the rest of the mechanism turns, and any intervention that compromises them — including well-intentioned interventions that begin downstream and attempt to backfill trust through branding or institutional assertion — seizes the engine.

2.5 Case Vignette: ACT UP and the Parallel Track

The AIDS Coalition to Unleash Power, active in its founding form from 1987 through the early 1990s, remains the most thoroughly documented contemporary case of the cascade executed in sequence under existential pressure. The case is instructive because the cascade was followed not from theory but from the practical necessity of a community whose institutional allies had abandoned it and whose members were dying on a timeline that did not permit sequencing errors.

Listening and Legitimacy (Steps 1–2) were established through die-in actions and community meetings documented by Schulman (2021) in Let the Record Show. The die-ins functioned not primarily as protest but as signal reception — public demonstrations of the lived condition whose specifics the institutional response had been failing to register. Legitimacy accrued through the accuracy and specificity of ACT UP’s listening: the coalition’s published demands were grounded in the actual clinical and social conditions of people with AIDS rather than in advocacy-organization generalities. Trust (Step 3) emerged from the community’s investment in its own survival; unlike coalitions manufactured through professional organizing, ACT UP’s trust substrate was produced by the alignment of the coalition’s interests with the immediate material interests of its members.

Sources and Truth (Steps 4–5) were produced through the Treatment and Data Committee, whose members — many with no prior medical training — became expert readers of the clinical literature, cultivated relationships with dissident clinicians and FDA scientists, and published community-accessible syntheses of the research frontier through what eventually became the Treatment Action Group. Art (Step 6) was produced through Gran Fury, the SILENCE = DEATH Project, and the NAMES Project AIDS Memorial Quilt; each functioned as aesthetic work whose authority was sufficient to render institutional refusal visible as refusal. Gravity (Step 7) — cultural reach beyond the directly affected community — was produced by the art cylinder’s success: the NAMES Project Quilt, displayed on the National Mall in 1987 and in subsequent years, made visible to the broader American public a scale of loss that the previous six years of epidemiological reporting had failed to register.

Funding, Institutions, and Policy (Steps 8–10) followed. Foundation and eventually federal capital flowed to AIDS research at scales unprecedented for an infectious disease affecting marginalized communities. The FDA’s parallel-track drug approval mechanism, the AIDS Clinical Trials Group structural reforms, and the Ryan White CARE Act (1990) represented institutional and policy change produced by a coalition that had executed every prior stage in sequence.

ACT UP executed this cascade in approximately five years under conditions of acute mass mortality. The Switchboard’s claim that the cascade requires sequential execution is not contradicted by the compressed timeline; it is confirmed by it. ACT UP demonstrates the minimum time required when the upstream stages are executed in full. Interventions that attempt to compress the timeline further by skipping stages do not accelerate the cascade; they break it. The operational question the Switchboard proposes to answer is whether mechanism can produce the sequenced execution that existential pressure produced in ACT UP’s case — whether, in other words, a community need not be dying in order to execute the cascade well.

3. Prior Literature and the Empirical Floor

The mechanism proposed in §2 sits in specific relation to four published bodies of work. Each is load-bearing: if the cited finding is wrong, a specific component of the architecture fails. The review is deliberately narrow. Broader intellectual provenance is gestured at in §3.5 and developed in a separate working document available on request.

3.1 Kuran (1995): Preference Falsification as Rational Equilibrium

Kuran’s Private Truths, Public Lies provides the economic formalization of the mechanism this paper treats as substrate. Preference falsification, as Kuran defines it, is the systematic misrepresentation of private preferences in response to perceived social pressures. The equilibrium is stable under specified conditions: the cost of first-mover defection exceeds individual willingness to bear it; institutional rewards are conditioned on continued performance of public claims; the information environment cannot reliably distinguish sincere from performed claims.

Kuran also specifies conditions under which preference cascades do occur — the sudden collapse of apparent consensus that accompanies revolutions, social movements, and the rapid shift of previously unsayable positions into mainstream discourse. Cascades are possible but not inevitable; they require a critical mass of defectors whose aggregate visibility lowers the cost for subsequent defectors. The Switchboard is proposed here as a mechanism for manufacturing the conditions under which cascades become likely rather than waiting for the spontaneous emergence of critical mass. Specifically, Steps 1–3 (Listening, Legitimacy, Trust) are designed to lower the first-mover cost that Kuran identifies as the equilibrium’s load-bearing feature.

What the paper takes from Kuran: the formal mechanism substrate, the stability conditions, and the identification of first-mover cost as the actionable variable. What the paper does not take: claims about specific historical cases, which Kuran develops at length and which are adjacent to but not constitutive of the argument made here.

3.2 Bail (2021): The Empirical Floor

Bail’s Breaking the Social Media Prism and the preceding program of research at the Duke Polarization Lab (Bail et al., 2018; Bail, 2022) establish an empirical finding this paper treats as the floor beneath its intervention logic. The finding, stated precisely: corrective information delivered to populations under conditions of identity threat tends to entrench rather than revise prior beliefs. The information-deficit model of persuasion — that people hold wrong beliefs because they lack correct information, and that supplying correct information corrects the belief — is contextually backwards under the conditions Bail’s experiments specify.

Bail’s finding is narrower than the paper’s needs and must be stated carefully to remain accurate. The finding is not that information transfer fails categorically. It is that corrective information fails predictably when the belief to be corrected is bound up with identity and when the information-transfer context is perceived as adversarial. This is an important distinction: the paper’s argument requires the narrower claim, not the broader one.

The Switchboard’s architecture is constructed to operate within Bail’s empirical floor rather than in denial of it. Steps 1–3 (Listening, Legitimacy, Trust) are designed to produce the non-adversarial context under which information transfer becomes possible; the art cylinder (Steps 6–7) is designed to deliver integrative content through forms that bypass the identity-threat response Bail documents; the mutual aid cylinder (Step 8) produces the material substrate under which populations are not defending scarce self-concept resources against adversarial framing. The mechanism treats Bail’s finding as a constraint on intervention design, not a counsel of despair.

The methodological pathway Bail’s lab has developed — experimental social psychology applied to information environments at scale — is the pathway through which this paper’s claims should be tested. The validation roadmap in §7 treats Duke SCAI and the Polarization Lab as the primary academic validation partner for that reason. The author cites Bail here because his finding shapes the mechanism, and hopes to work with him because the validation infrastructure he has built is the infrastructure the paper’s empirical claims require.

3.3 Thaler (2008, 2015): Cognitive Value and Choice Architecture

Thaler and Sunstein’s Nudge (2008) and Thaler’s Misbehaving (2015) establish the canonical framework for choice architecture: the observation that small, structural features of decision environments systematically influence behavior, and that designing these features carefully can produce welfare-improving outcomes without constraining individual choice. The framework earned Thaler the 2017 Nobel Prize in Economics and defines the field in which this paper sits.

The paper’s title is a deliberate signal of lineage. “Cognitive value” is Thaler’s construct, and this paper’s proposal extends it in a specific direction. Nudge operates primarily at the level of individual choice architecture — retirement enrollment defaults, cafeteria layout, organ donation opt-outs. The Cognitive Value Protocol proposes that the same logic applies at the level of cultural and institutional infrastructure, where the relevant “choice architecture” is not the design of a single decision but the design of the trust environment within which many decisions are made. Where Thaler’s nudge treats information as given and designs the presentation, this paper treats trust as the bottleneck and designs the sequencing.

The relationship is additive, not competitive.

The relationship is additive, not competitive. Nudge interventions presuppose the existence of a trust environment in which the nudge can operate; the Switchboard is proposed as the mechanism by which that trust environment is produced at scale. Where a nudge assumes a decision-maker who trusts the institution presenting the choice, the Switchboard addresses the population for whom that trust has collapsed and without which no nudge will work.

What the paper takes from Thaler: the core insight that behavioral change occurs through structural design rather than moral exhortation, and the specific vocabulary of “cognitive value” that the paper’s title adopts and extends. What the paper does not take: the strong libertarian-paternalism framing, which Thaler and Sunstein develop in Nudge and which is adjacent to but not required for this paper’s argument. The paper’s operational register is engineering, not political philosophy.

A note on related behavioral-economics work: Rory Sutherland, whose Alchemy (2019) develops choice-architecture intuitions in a practitioner-facing register, has been a named advisor to the author’s practice since 2020. His influence on the operational vocabulary of the framework is real and is disclosed here. The peer-reviewed economics anchor remains Thaler.

3.4 Ostrom (1990): Commons Governance

Ostrom’s Governing the Commons and the subsequent body of work on polycentric governance (Ostrom 1990, 2005, 2009) establish the design principles under which commons institutions have been observed to sustain themselves across generations. Her eight principles — clearly defined boundaries; congruence between rules and local conditions; collective-choice arrangements; monitoring; graduated sanctions; conflict-resolution mechanisms; recognition of self-governance rights; nested enterprises — were derived from empirical study of durable commons (fisheries, irrigation systems, forests) and have subsequently been tested across a wide range of institutional forms.

The Switchboard is proposed as a cognitive-infrastructure commons, and the architecture specified in §6 is a direct application of Ostrom’s principles to the governance of Switchboard instances. The applicability of commons-governance logic to cognitive infrastructure is not obvious and is not asserted here. It is a claim the paper makes and is prepared to defend: cognitive infrastructure shares the structural features that make Ostrom’s principles applicable — collective production, shared appropriation, the possibility of capture, the necessity of exclusion rules — and the failure modes of cognitive-infrastructure institutions track the failure modes Ostrom identified in physical commons. The analogy is tested operationally through the architecture in §6 and falsified if the architecture fails.

What the paper takes from Ostrom: the eight principles as the affirmative specification of the Switchboard’s governance, the concept of polycentric governance as the target state of the federation, and the empirical methodology of deriving governance principles from the observed durability of institutions rather than from first-principle prescription. What the paper does not take: specific empirical claims about fisheries or irrigation systems, which are the source material for the principles but are not analogically transferred to the cognitive-infrastructure domain. 4. Downstream Clinical Efficiency

The prior literature establishes the theoretical and empirical floor. The question this section addresses is whether the integration-capacity bottleneck can be quantified — whether the cognitive work currently performed inefficiently by the clinical system can be measured with sufficient precision to justify investment in upstream cognitive infrastructure that would perform it more efficiently.

The proposed metric is Downstream Clinical Efficiency (DCE): the ratio between the cost of installing critical-integration vocabulary (trauma fluency, DARVO recognition, boundary grammar, coercive-control literacy) through clinical intervention versus the cost of installing the same vocabulary through upstream cognitive infrastructure — documentary films, long-form journalism, narrative fiction, public-facing scholarship — delivered through the Crankshaft’s art and journalism cylinders.

4.1 Clinical Precedent

The intuition that community-level cognitive infrastructure performs clinical work is not new. It is grounded in the Global Mental Health literature, particularly Vikram Patel’s research on community-based interventions in low-resource settings (Patel et al., 2010, 2017) and Dixon Chibanda’s Friendship Bench program in Zimbabwe (Chibanda et al., 2016), which demonstrates that trained lay counselors delivering problem-solving therapy on wooden benches achieve outcomes comparable to formal clinical interventions at a fraction of the cost. The 2018 and 2021 Lancet Commissions on Global Mental Health (Patel et al., 2018; Patel et al., 2021) synthesize the evidentiary base for community-level intervention as a substantive rather than supplementary component of mental-health infrastructure.

The DCE argument is an extension of this established logic into the upstream cognitive-infrastructure domain. Where the Friendship Bench demonstrates that trained lay counselors can deliver clinical-grade intervention, the DCE argument proposes that well-produced cultural and journalistic artifacts can deliver vocabulary-installation at a scale and cost that makes upstream pre-processing of clinical load economically rational. The claim is falsifiable under the terms specified in §8.5.

4.2 Fermi Estimate

An initial Fermi estimate, to be hardened through the validation roadmap in §7, is as follows. The inputs and sources are specified so the estimate can be reproduced or refuted by any reader.

Prevalence input. The National Intimate Partner and Sexual Violence Survey (NISVS) estimates that approximately 36.4% of US women and 33.6% of US men have experienced contact sexual violence, physical violence, and/or stalking by an intimate partner in their lifetime (Smith, S.G., et al., 2018 — NISVS Data Brief). Coercive-control prevalence outside physical violence is measured far less consistently — Hamberger, Larsen, and Lehrner (2017) identify 22 distinct measures and definitions of coercive-control victimization in use — so no single coercive-control range is relied on here. Applied conservatively to the US adult population of approximately 258 million (US Census Bureau, 2023), the NISVS lifetime figures alone yield an exposed population exceeding 75 million. Treatment reach falls far short of that population: 23.0% of US adults received any mental health treatment in the past year, and even among adults with diagnosable mental illness, only about half (50.6%) received past-year treatment (SAMHSA, 2023 NSDUH; NIMH analysis of 2022 NSDUH data). The \~50 million untreated-vocabulary-gap figure used below is consistent with the conjunction of these inputs.

Clinical cost input. Average out-of-pocket psychotherapy session cost in the United States ranges from $100 to $200 per session for licensed professionals in 2023–2024 (American Psychological Association practice-rate guidance, 2023; SimplePractice practitioner benchmarks, 2024). Session counts required for trauma-focused psychoeducation prior to active treatment are estimated in the clinical literature at 6 to 12 sessions for Cognitive Processing Therapy orientation (Resick, Monson, & Chard, 2016) and comparable ranges for Trauma-Focused CBT. The $150 × 8 session figure used here ($1,200 per installation) sits at the midpoint of these ranges.

Distribution input. Documentary audience reach at the scale contemplated is empirically defensible. Netflix’s The Social Dilemma (2020) reached an estimated 38 million households in its first 28 days (Spangler, 2020); Ken Burns’s tentpole PBS releases have drawn cumulative audiences in the 33–39 million range (The Vietnam War, 2017: 33.8 million viewers across the premiere run and 39 million unique viewers including encores; Country Music, 2019: 34.5 million unique viewers; PBS viewership reporting, 2017–2019). Adam Curtis’s HyperNormalisation (2016) was released direct to BBC iPlayer; the BBC has published no viewership figure for it, and none is claimed here. A one-million-viewer floor for a well-executed, well-distributed documentary artifact is conservative against these benchmarks.

Conversion input. Media-delivered mental-health-literacy interventions have documented effects on knowledge, attitudes, and help-seeking behavior (Jorm, 2012; Kelly, Jorm, & Wright, 2007), but that literature does not supply a settled conversion-rate band for vocabulary installation, and none is asserted here. The 10% rate used below is an explicit assumption submitted for independent actuarial and academic review, and the falsification conditions in §8.5 apply to it directly.

Production cost input. Documentary production budgets for works of the quality and distribution scale contemplated range from $500,000 (well-executed independent feature documentary) to $2 million (streaming-platform commissioned feature documentary). This band is the author’s estimate from production practice dating to 2013, stated as such — published industry surveys do not report budget bands at this specificity — and it is submitted to the planned actuarial review with the other inputs.

4.3 DCE Calculation

Conservative application: 1 million viewers × 10% conversion × $1,200 avoided clinical cost = $120M avoided clinical load. Against $500K–$2M production cost, the raw DCE ratio is 60:1 to 240:1. Discounting by 90% for attribution uncertainty, temporal decay of effect, partial rather than complete vocabulary installation, and the risk that a given artifact never reaches the one-million-viewer floor, the defensible floor is 6:1 to 24:1. The undiscounted ratio is an order of magnitude above standard preventive public-health benchmarks (Masters et al., 2017: a median return on investment of 14.3:1 across 52 included studies); even the heavily discounted floor brackets that benchmark rather than falling below it.

The review is expected to challenge inputs the author has treated as directionally sufficient, propose alternative sourcing where citations are insufficiently rigorous, and either harden the estimate against the class of numerical objection the Fermi calculation is most vulnerable to, or flag specific inputs as insufficiently defensible for inclusion. 4.5 What the Estimate Establishes

What the Fermi estimate establishes, directionally, is that the cost structure of upstream cognitive infrastructure relative to downstream clinical intervention is not a matter of degrees. It is a matter of orders of magnitude. The Crankshaft is proposed not as an alternative to clinical intervention but as the upstream pre-processing layer whose absence forces the clinical system to bear costs it was never designed to bear and cannot efficiently bear. The DCE ratio is what turns this proposition from theoretical claim into testable hypothesis.

5. The Hafiz Societal Repair Index

5.1 Purpose and Status

The Hafiz Societal Repair Index (HSRI) is proposed as a composite, real-time, open-standard index for measuring the condition of the integration-capacity substrate in a defined population. It stands in the same relation to this paper’s mechanism as the Richter scale stands to seismology or the Jin-Hafiz Disinformation Index (Jin et al., 2021) stands to information-hazard research: a publicly available measurement framework that any research program or institution may adopt, extend, or contest. Its construction follows the compositional discipline of the OECD–JRC Handbook on Constructing Composite Indicators (OECD/JRC, 2008), and resistance to Goodhart’s law — the regularity that a measure degrades once it becomes a target (Goodhart, 1984) — is treated as a first-order design constraint rather than an acknowledged limitation: the capture-index dimension exists to detect precisely the gaming behavior Goodhart predicts.

The HSRI is offered as an open standard. The specific operationalization, weighting, and integration algorithms by which HSRI outputs are used to adjust Crankshaft cylinder timing are the proprietary methodology of Aux Labs, released under licensing terms to academic and institutional partners. This separation follows standard practice in measurement science: the PHQ-9 defines its scale openly while the clinical programs built around it remain proprietary to their operators; the Richter scale is universally cited while specific earthquake-response protocols vary by institution.

5.2 Why a Composite Index

Single-dimension measures of public trust, institutional legitimacy, or civic engagement have a long history and a longer record of failure. Gallup and Pew have been measuring institutional trust in the United States since the 1970s and have documented an almost uninterrupted decline across every major institution over five decades (Brenan, 2023; Pew Research Center, 2022). The measurements are accurate. The question they cannot answer is why, because single-dimension trust measurement conflates multiple distinct phenomena — preference falsification, integration lag, coalition fragmentation, and capture — into a single downward line.

The HSRI decomposes the integration-capacity substrate into four dimensions, each measuring a distinct phenomenon and each corresponding to a specific failure mode the Crankshaft is designed to address. Movement on any single dimension indicates a specific mechanism failure and prescribes a specific corrective reallocation upstream in the cascade. The four dimensions are preference honesty, integration velocity, coalition breadth, and capture index.

5.3 Dimension 1 — Preference Honesty

Preference honesty measures the delta between privately held preferences and publicly expressed preferences in a defined population on a defined topic. This is the direct operationalization of Kuran’s preference-falsification thesis (Kuran, 1995) and of Havel’s living within the lie (Havel, 1978). A population in which private and public preferences align is a population in which individuals can afford to say what they think; a population in which they diverge is a population in which the cost of first-mover defection exceeds individual willingness to bear it. The divergence is the bottleneck.

The proposed instrumentation is paired-instrument surveys, administered to matched samples under differing disclosure conditions. The methodological precedent is the list-experiment tradition in political science (Blair & Imai, 2012; Rosenfeld, Imai, & Shapiro, 2016), used to measure socially sensitive preferences from racial attitudes to vote choice.

The HSRI preference-honesty dimension is proposed as PH = 1 − |P_private − P_public| / P_max, where P_private is the aggregate preference expressed under conditions of guaranteed anonymity, P_public is the aggregate preference expressed under conditions of social visibility, and P_max is the theoretical maximum divergence for the measured instrument. PH approaches 1 as private and public preferences converge. PH below threshold τ_PH indicates a Pretend Era equilibrium on the measured topic; corrective action prescribed is upstream reallocation to Steps 1 and 2.

5.4 Dimension 2 — Integration Velocity

Integration velocity measures the time lag between the emergence of a critical-integration vocabulary in artistic or journalistic production (Step 6, Step 5) and its appearance in clinical and institutional settings (Step 9). This is the direct operationalization of the DCE thesis: that cognitive infrastructure delivered through art and journalism is a pre-processing layer for the clinical system, and the efficiency of the mechanism can be measured by how quickly vocabulary travels from upstream stages to downstream sites where it is applied.

The proposed instrumentation is linguistic drift analysis applied to clinical and institutional corpora. The methodological precedent is the diachronic word-embedding literature (Hamilton, Leskovec, & Jurafsky, 2016; Kulkarni et al., 2015). Electronic health record data, where available under appropriate research agreements, provides the downstream corpus; publicly archived journalism and cultural production provides the upstream corpus.

IV = 1 / Δt, where Δt is the median time in months between the first appearance of a target vocabulary term (coercive control, DARVO, moral injury, ambiguous loss) in the upstream corpus and the 50th-percentile adoption of that term in the downstream corpus. The median lag between academic publication of these terms and their appearance in mass clinical practice has been estimated at 17 years (Morris, Wooding, & Grant, 2011 — their title: “The answer is 17 years, what is the question”). The HSRI’s integration-velocity dimension aims to reduce this lag.

5.5 Dimension 3 — Coalition Breadth

Coalition breadth measures the cross-demographic distribution of the population that has adopted a critical-integration vocabulary. The claim is that mechanism success requires not only fast integration but broad integration: a vocabulary that moves quickly through a narrow demographic has not solved the integration-capacity problem, because the populations most exposed to the Pretend Era’s downstream consequences are not necessarily the populations most exposed to documentary cinema, long-form journalism, or graduate-seminar reading.

The proposed instrumentation is population-level survey measurement of vocabulary familiarity and application, cross-tabulated against demographic categories including income, education, region, age, and media-consumption pattern. The methodological precedent is the American National Election Studies (ANES) tradition and the General Social Survey (GSS), adapted to measure vocabulary-level penetration rather than policy preferences.

CB is defined as the demographic variance-adjusted breadth of adoption across measured categories. CB below threshold τ_CB indicates vocabulary penetration is demographically restricted; corrective action prescribed is Step 6 reallocation toward forms and channels designed for populations underrepresented in the current documentary audience.

5.6 Dimension 4 — Capture Index

The capture index measures the correlation between sponsor identity and topic distribution in funded work across a rolling window, relative to a null distribution generated from unsponsored baseline production. The capture index is the direct telemetry for the firewall’s self-enforcement claim: if the firewall is working, CI remains near zero regardless of sponsor volume; if the firewall is failing, CI rises and prescribes immediate corrective action.

The methodological precedent is the funding-influence analysis literature in medicine and science policy (Lexchin et al., 2003; Bes-Rastrollo et al., 2013), which has documented measurable correlation between industry funding and research-outcome direction in pharmaceuticals and nutrition science.

CI = ρ(S, T) − ρ_null, where ρ(S, T) is the correlation between sponsor identity and topic distribution across a rolling window and ρ_null is the baseline correlation expected under random sponsor-topic assignment. CI approaching 0 indicates the firewall is holding; CI above τ_CI triggers the capture-resistance response — sponsor reallocation, publication of the anomaly under commons-governance transparency principles (Ostrom, 1990), and in severe cases, sponsor dissociation.

5.7 Threshold Parameters

Each HSRI dimension is measured against a threshold τ whose value determines when corrective action is triggered. The four thresholds — τ_PH, τ_IV, τ_CB, τ_CI — are not proposed here as settled values. They are parameters to be calibrated during academic validation partnership. The author defers operational parameterization to peer review rather than asserting it, on the grounds that the defensibility of any specific threshold depends on population, topic, and measurement conditions that validation partners are better positioned to specify.

The calibration protocol proposed for validation partnership is: (1) selection of a defined population and topic area; (2) baseline measurement of all four dimensions in that population; (3) identification of historical integration successes and failures in comparable populations for which dimension values can be retrospectively estimated; (4) threshold placement at the values that best discriminate historical success from historical failure; (5) validation against a held-out test case. This is standard measurement-science practice and is offered as a collaborative research program rather than a deliverable.

5.8 The Composite

The four dimensions are integrated into a single composite HSRI score according to a weighting algorithm whose specific parameters are reserved as proprietary methodology under the licensing terms described in §5.1. The public specification of the composite is HSRI = w₁(PH) + w₂(IV) + w₃(CB) − w₄(CI), where w₁ through w₄ are weights summing to a normalized total; PH, IV, and CB contribute positively to the composite; and CI contributes negatively (capture is a subtraction from repair).

The rationale for proprietary weighting is operational, not commercial: inappropriate weighting of a public-standard index produces misleading signals, and Aux Labs retains calibration authority to prevent the HSRI from being reverse-engineered into a sponsor-friendly reputation-management instrument. The dimensions remain public; the integration remains accountable. This is the same structure under which the Jin-Hafiz Disinformation Index was released in 2021.

6. Institutional Architecture

6.1 Two Institutions, Two Roles

A recurring ambiguity in prior drafts of this framework has been the confusion between Aux Labs and the Switchboard. This section establishes the distinction clearly.

Aux Labs LLC is a for-profit research lab drawing on mechanism design, signaling theory, and behavioral science. It developed the Switchboard protocol, the Hafiz Societal Repair Index, its applied methodology, and the theoretical framework described in this paper. Its function is protocol stewardship: developing, refining, licensing, and governing the use of the Switchboard across instances. Under the architecture specified below, Aux Labs is the first governor of the Switchboard protocol, under conditions of graduated sovereignty that anticipate its eventual evolution from custodial authority to one node within a polycentric network of peer Switchboards.

A Switchboard is an operational instance of the protocol applied to a specific population, geography, or domain of social tension. Each Switchboard is a four-cylinder engine (journalism, art, mutual aid, public accountability) firing against the ten-stage cascade specified in §2. Switchboards are not franchises of Aux Labs. They are sovereign institutions operating under a shared governance protocol, licensed and monitored by Aux Labs during the custodial phase and federated into peer-governance as the conditions specified in §6.3 are met.

This distinction matters because conflation produces governance incoherence. If Aux Labs governs both the protocol and each Switchboard directly, the architecture recapitulates the ownership-participation divergence identified as a central capture mechanism of cultural-commons failure. If Aux Labs governs only the protocol, while Switchboards govern themselves within the protocol’s constraints, the architecture satisfies Ostrom’s eighth principle (nested enterprises) by design rather than by assertion.

6.2 Ostrom’s Eight Principles, Applied Affirmatively

Each principle is applied below to the Switchboard architecture as a structural protection against a specific capture pattern observed in prior commons-institution failures.

Principle 1 — Clearly defined boundaries. Switchboard participants are defined by formal affiliation (staff, commissioned contributors, Foundations Fellows, community members receiving mutual aid services) and beneficiaries are distinguishable from contributors. Value produced through collective labor accrues through specified channels rather than through informal claims.

Principle 2 — Congruence between rules and local conditions. Each Switchboard instance operates under governance rules adapted to its specific population and domain, within protocol constraints that specify what must be preserved across instances and what may vary.

Principle 3 — Collective-choice arrangements. Participants affected by Switchboard governance have standing to participate in modifying it. This is operationalized through the graduated-sovereignty architecture specified in §6.3.

Principle 4 — Monitoring. The HSRI provides continuous real-time monitoring of the four dimensions on which the mechanism is proposed to operate, with specific attention to the capture index as the primary signal of firewall failure.

Principle 5 — Graduated sanctions. Protocol violations are addressed through a graduated response that begins with internal correction, escalates to public documentation through the Capture Index, and in severe cases results in license revocation and dissociation.

Principle 6 — Conflict-resolution mechanisms. Disputes within and between Switchboards are addressed through specified arbitration procedures documented in the licensing protocol.

Principle 7 — Recognition of rights to self-governance. The graduated-sovereignty architecture specifies the operational conditions under which protocol-governance authority transitions from Aux Labs to the Switchboard federation itself.

Principle 8 — Nested enterprises. Aux Labs governs the protocol; Switchboards govern their operations; local cylinders govern their production. Each governance tier operates within the constraints of the tier above, and no single tier controls the entire stack.

6.3 Graduated Sovereignty

The author’s authority over the protocol transitions through three stages triggered by observable operational conditions rather than by time or founder discretion. These protocol-level stages are distinct from the instance-level maturation tiers by which an individual Switchboard gains local autonomy; the stages govern who holds authority over the protocol itself, while the tiers govern how much autonomy a given instance exercises under it.

Stage One — Custodial Authority. Aux Labs governs the protocol under founder authority, with licensing terms specified and applied uniformly across instances. This is the current stage.

Stage Two — Collaborative Governance. Triggered by the successful operation of at least five independent Switchboard instances across multiple domains of social tension, accompanied by successful academic validation of the HSRI dimensions and the DCE ratio through peer-reviewed publication. At this stage, the Switchboard federation assumes shared governance of the protocol, with Aux Labs retaining founder dividend and the right of protocol amendment proposal but ceding the right of unilateral amendment.

Stage Three — Polycentric Federation. Triggered by the Switchboard federation reaching approximately $10M annual aggregate revenue and the successful operation of at least five national-scale projects under federation governance. At this stage, Aux Labs becomes one node within a polycentric network; the founder retains the dividend specified in §6.5 but holds no remaining governance authority over the federation itself.

The trigger conditions are defensible proposals, not empirically calibrated values. Independent validation is required before any Switchboard transitions on their basis. The founder’s exit is built into the design rather than hoped for.

6.4 The Sponsor Firewall

The firewall is the architectural feature that makes capture economically irrational. Foundation capital enters upstream of Step 6 (Art), funding the first three cascade stages (Listening, Legitimacy, Trust) and the artistic ideation at Step 6. Brand and sponsor capital enters only at Step 8 (Funding), after Step 6 has been independently ideated through non-commercial mechanisms. The separation is not a governance preference. It is a claim about how the two forms of capital actually operate: foundation capital is productive upstream and unproductive downstream; brand capital is productive downstream and contaminative upstream. Where the regimes are crossed, the characteristic failure modes of each appear predictably.

The firewall is proposed as self-enforcing through two mechanisms. Transactional: a sponsor who attempts to redirect upstream work destroys the legitimacy they came for, making capture economically irrational for the sponsor. Commons-governance: violations are published through the Capture Index and cost the sponsor the legitimacy they sought to acquire, making capture reputationally costly. The full operational specification of the firewall is proprietary methodology released under licensing terms; the architectural claim and its falsifiability conditions are public (§8.5, §8.6).

6.5 The Founder Dividend and the University-Hospital Model

The founder’s economic participation in the Switchboard federation is structured on the model of the university-hospital relationship rather than the founder-startup relationship. The founder retains a specified dividend on the protocol’s continued operation — set at a level sufficient to reward the work of developing the framework and to prevent the founder from facing economic pressure to extract from the commons — but holds no equity claim on individual Switchboard operations and no governance authority after Stage Three.

This distinction is specified here rather than left to the licensing documents because the failure mode it prevents is specific: founders whose long-term economic interest remains tied to the commons they founded face ongoing incentive to extract value from that commons, and this incentive is one of the mechanisms through which cultural institutions that begin with counter-hegemonic intent arrive at the ownership-participation divergence that destroys them. The dividend is contractually severable from governance, and its magnitude is documented in the founding instruments of each Switchboard.

6.6 Scope Conditions

The architecture specified above is proposed, not proven. Several questions remain open. The trigger conditions of §6.3 are defensible proposals awaiting independent validation. The self-funding mechanism assumes a Switchboard federation operating at approximately $10M annual aggregate revenue can be sustained through licensing, agency fees, cultural-production revenue, and foundation core support; this assumption is to be tested through the operation of the first Switchboard instances. The AI-integration requirement specified as a Stage Three trigger is the subject of ongoing research addressed in forthcoming work. The architecture is proposed for the class of institutions where value is produced through collective labor experienced as shared, governance is informal, and legal ownership is otherwise concentrated; its applicability to other institutional forms is a question for future research.

The architecture is proposed here. It will be tested by operation. It will be revised by what operation reveals. Null results in graduated sovereignty are as valuable as positive results, because the architecture’s claim is falsifiable only if its failures are documented.

8.1 Why This Section Exists

The author has spent the years since 2008 developing the framework described in this paper. Some of that development has been through documented successes; more has been through operational failures (including the Texas Cave Management Association project documented in §2.6) and through working inside institutions that subsequently failed in the specific ways the framework diagnoses. The failures have produced the data. Each falsifiability commitment below is backed by prior experience of what it looks like when that specific claim fails in practice.

8.2 Commitment 1 — The Mechanism Coherence Claim

The claim: The Switchboard–Crankshaft mechanism specified in §2 is coherent with the prior literature reviewed in §3. Specifically: the cascade sequencing is consistent with Kuran’s analysis of preference-cascade conditions; the art and mutual-aid cylinders are designed to operate within Bail’s empirical floor rather than against it; the cognitive-value framing extends Thaler’s choice-architecture logic to the infrastructure scale; the governance architecture is a direct application of Ostrom’s design principles.

Falsification conditions: (i) demonstration by a scholar in any of these four traditions that the Switchboard architecture contradicts, rather than extends, the cited finding; (ii) demonstration that the Switchboard architecture misreads the cited work in a way that materially affects the mechanism’s operation; (iii) demonstration that a prior mechanism-design framework has achieved the same integration of these constraints, invalidating the contribution claim.

Retraction commitment: Upon demonstration of any of the above, the paper retracts the specific coherence claim and either revises the architecture to accommodate the corrected reading or acknowledges the limitation explicitly in subsequent drafts. If prior art is established, authorship credit for the mechanism is reassigned accordingly.

8.3 Commitment 2 — Cascade Sequentiality

The claim: The ten-stage Switchboard cascade is unidirectional in its legitimacy flow, such that interventions entering at downstream stages without upstream infrastructure in place will produce predictable failure modes rather than accelerated outcomes. The ordering is proposed as a physical property of the mechanism rather than a governance preference.

Falsification conditions: (i) identification of an intervention that entered the cascade at a downstream stage (Step 6 or later) without the upstream stages in place and that achieved durable success over at least three years; (ii) identification of a sequence ordering different from the proposed ten stages that produces equivalent or superior outcomes in matched domains; (iii) demonstration that the failure modes documented in the Texas Cave Management Association vignette (§2.6) and in VICE’s 2023 collapse are attributable to causes unrelated to stage sequencing.

Retraction commitment: The sequentiality claim is revised to permit alternative orderings under specified conditions, or the cascade is revised to reflect the correct ordering. The stronger form of the claim — that cascade violation is “a physical property of the mechanism” — is the most vulnerable to falsification and would be replaced by a weaker empirical generalization if the evidence warrants.

8.4 Commitment 3 — HSRI Dimensional Independence

The claim: The four HSRI dimensions — preference honesty, integration velocity, coalition breadth, and capture index — measure structurally distinct phenomena. Their decomposition is not an analytical convenience but a claim about the underlying structure of the integration-capacity substrate.

Falsification conditions: (i) factor-analytic demonstration, conducted by an independent research team, that the four dimensions collapse to a single-factor or two-factor solution under standard psychometric testing; (ii) demonstration that a single dimension is sufficient to predict DCE and cascade-operation outcomes, making the other three redundant; (iii) demonstration that the four dimensions as operationalized in §§5.3–5.6 measure artifacts of the instrumentation rather than the phenomena they name.

Retraction commitment: Upon demonstration of dimensional collapse, the HSRI is revised to the empirically supported dimensionality, with the composite formula adjusted accordingly. If the four-dimension claim is empirically indefensible, the framework reverts to whatever dimensionality the data supports, and the theoretical claim about integration-capacity decomposition is retracted or weakened.

8.5 Commitment 4 — Downstream Clinical Efficiency

The claim: Cognitive infrastructure delivered through the art and journalism cylinders (Steps 5–6) produces measurable reductions in downstream clinical load at a ratio whose defensibility floor is 6:1 to 24:1 after conservative discounting, per the Fermi estimate in §4.3.

Falsification conditions: (i) controlled study demonstrating that populations exposed to Crankshaft-delivered cognitive infrastructure show no statistically significant reduction in clinical load relative to matched controls; (ii) demonstration that the Fermi estimate’s inputs (prevalence, clinical cost, distribution, conversion, production cost) are miscalibrated by orders of magnitude in ways that collapse the ratio below the defensibility threshold when corrected; (iii) demonstration that the intervention produces cost-shifting rather than cost-reduction — that apparent reductions in clinical load represent cases moved into untreated populations rather than cases that no longer require treatment.

Retraction commitment: The DCE argument is retracted if the ratio fails to clear the defensibility floor under independent validation. The upstream-infrastructure-as-pre-processing claim that rests on DCE is revised to reflect whatever efficiency ratio the evidence supports, including zero. The author commits to publishing null or negative DCE results in the same venues as positive results.

8.6 Commitment 5 — Firewall Self-Enforcement

The claim: The sponsor firewall described in §6.4 makes capture economically irrational through a combination of transactional logic (the sponsor cannot redirect the work without destroying the asset they came for) and commons-governance logic (violations are published through the Capture Index and cost the sponsor the legitimacy they sought to acquire).

Falsification conditions: (i) documented instance of a sponsor successfully extracting editorial influence at Step 8 without forfeiting associated legitimacy gains — capture occurring and remaining profitable; (ii) sustained sponsor-topic correlation above threshold τ_CI without triggering the specified capture-resistance responses, indicating the enforcement mechanism is decorative rather than functional; (iii) demonstration that artists or journalists operating inside the firewall’s sponsor-free zone exhibit preemptive self-censorship patterns statistically indistinguishable from commercially commissioned work.

Retraction commitment: The firewall’s self-enforcement claim is retracted if any of the above obtain. The architecture either adopts additional enforcement mechanisms (contractual, regulatory, or governance-based) or acknowledges that the firewall is a policy preference requiring active enforcement rather than a structurally self-enforcing mechanism.

8.7 Commitment 6 — Graduated Sovereignty

The claim: The terrain-gated transitions specified in §6.3 — founder custodianship to collaborative governance to polycentric federation, triggered by observable operational conditions rather than by time or founder discretion — produce commons durability superior to alternative governance models (founder-perpetual, time-gated, or discretionary-succession) in comparable institutions.

Falsification conditions: (i) observation, over sufficient operational time, that Switchboard instances governed under the graduated-sovereignty architecture collapse at rates indistinguishable from comparable institutions governed under alternative models; (ii) demonstration that the specific trigger conditions proposed in §6.3 are not the correct conditions for authority transition; (iii) observation that founder-exit under the specified architecture produces the ownership-participation divergence pathology.

Retraction commitment: The graduated-sovereignty architecture is the most temporally extended claim in this paper, and its falsifiability is therefore the most deferred. The author commits to publishing the operational record of each transition — successful and failed — as it occurs. If the architecture fails under operation, it is revised or replaced rather than defended.

8.8 Commitments to Publication

The author commits, in advance, to publishing the following under the conditions specified. Null results: any of the above falsification conditions, observed under independent validation, will be published in the same venue as the original claim. Null DCE results will appear in American Psychologist or equivalent clinical venues; null HSRI dimensional results will appear in the psychometric literature; null firewall or governance results will appear in the commons-governance literature.

Partial falsification: where a claim is partially rather than fully falsified — the HSRI holds three dimensions but not four, or the cascade is correctly ordered but not strictly sequential, or the firewall holds transactionally but not governance-wise — the partial result will be published with the specific weakening noted explicitly.

Operational failures: the Texas Cave Management Association vignette (§2.6) establishes the precedent. Future Switchboard instance failures, if they occur, will be documented with the same specificity. Timing failures: if the validation sequence specified in §7 stalls or collapses, the paper’s credibility claims degrade accordingly, and subsequent drafts will reflect the reduced validation status rather than asserting progress that has not occurred.

8.9 The Commitments as a Package

The six commitments above are not a defensive perimeter. They are the specification of what this paper’s claims would have to look like for the paper to be wrong. A reader encountering the framework for the first time can use this section as a map of the paper’s vulnerabilities and should feel free to test any of the six commitments against available evidence.

What the author will not do is reframe falsification as partial confirmation, move the goalposts when evidence accumulates against a specific claim, or defend an architecture that the evidence fails to support. This is stated plainly because the temptation to do any of those three is real, particularly for frameworks with multi-year development histories and operational investments, and the discipline of pre-committed falsifiability is the only reliable defense against the temptation.

The framework described in this paper may be correct, partially correct, or incorrect. Each of those outcomes is a legitimate result of the research program. What the author is committed to is ensuring that the result, whatever it is, is legible to the field rather than defended against it.

9. Conclusion

This paper has argued that the contemporary period of institutional failure across public-interest domains — journalism, clinical care, philanthropy, academic research, governmental capacity — is a discrete historical condition with a specific mechanism, a measurable substrate, documented downstream signatures, and a class of possible interventions. The condition is the Pretend Era. The mechanism is preference falsification at institutional scale, substrated cognitively by the architecture described in §1 and signatured materially in the deaths-of-despair epidemiology. The intervention is not exhortation, because exhortation in the presence of the first-mover cost asymmetry is either ignored or personally ruinous for the exhorter. The intervention is mechanism design — building institutional architecture whose rules produce the truth-telling behavior the Pretend Era equilibrium suppresses, by lowering the cost of truth-telling rather than demanding courage the equilibrium has already priced out.

The mechanism proposed is the Switchboard: a ten-stage cascade (the Trust-Repair Cascade) coordinated by four productive cylinders in offset combustion, gated by a sponsor firewall that routes foundation capital upstream and brand capital downstream, instrumented by a four-dimensional open-standard index, reproduced across a federation of sovereign instances under graduated sovereignty. The theoretical foundation is drawn from four bodies of published work — Kuran, Bail, Thaler, Ostrom — whose constraints the architecture is designed to satisfy simultaneously and whose methods provide the validation pathways under which the architecture’s claims can be tested.

The paper’s originality is not in any diagnostic element, each of which belongs to its cited scholar. The paper’s originality is in the operational architecture and the economic argument (Downstream Clinical Efficiency) that the architecture is proposed to justify. Both are falsifiable under conditions specified in §8.

The author is adjacent to academia, not inside it. This paper is offered in the tradition of practitioner-scholarship, with the understanding that its claims must earn their validation through the mechanisms the academic community has established for that purpose. The validation roadmap in §7 is the author’s proposal for how that earning occurs. The falsifiability commitments in §8 are the author’s advance specification of what retraction looks like if the evidence fails.

The framework was developed through practice dating to 2008 — including the specific operational failures catalogued in §2.6 and the institutional case studies developed in separate working papers. The failures produced the data on which the architecture is built. The data is not valuable because the failures were romantic. It is valuable because the failures were specific, the author bore their material costs, and the patterns they revealed are the patterns the framework is built to address.

The paper asks for evaluation, not agreement. The mechanism either satisfies the constraints reviewed in §3 or it does not. The HSRI either measures what it claims to measure or it does not. The DCE ratio either clears the defensibility floor or it does not. The firewall either self-enforces or it does not. The sovereignty architecture either produces the durability claimed or it does not. Each of these is a testable proposition, and the author is committed to publishing the results whichever way they go.

The work continues either way.
